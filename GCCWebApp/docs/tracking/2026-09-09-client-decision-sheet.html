What we found

The import already works the way your team hoped. Leave the Certificate # blank and the system assigns the next number and creates a new certificate. Enter an existing certificate’s number and the import updates that certificate instead of duplicating it — and before anything is saved you see a preview listing every field that would change, so a file can be abandoned at that point with nothing written.

The wording in the upload window contradicts that, which is what your team ran into. It is also inconsistent with the other import screen in the system, which describes the behaviour correctly. We are bringing the two into line and spelling out both cases.

One thing we should raise while you are here. A small number of certificate numbers in the current data appear on more than one record. If someone edits by import using one of those numbers, the system updates the first matching record, which may not be the one they intended. This comes from the historic data rather than the import itself, and it may never arise in practice. If your team is going to use the template routinely for edits we would suggest a check that pauses and warns when a number matches more than one certificate, rather than choosing quietly. Happy to look at that separately — it is not part of the work below.

The three decisions

Each one is genuinely a preference, not a technical constraint. Our recommendation is marked, and any combination works.

Decision 1

How far should the new tab reach?

Your team sees an Edit button in three places: on the tile card, in the list’s Actions column, and on the certificate detail page. All three run through the same underlying control, so they naturally change together.

☐ A — All three placesOur recommendation Edit always opens a new tab and always leaves your search behind it. One consistent behaviour to learn.
☐ B — The tile and list only Leaves the certificate detail page editing in place, as it does today. Fewer changes, but Edit then behaves differently depending on where you press it.

Decision 2

A fixed List default, or one that remembers?

The frustration described is having to switch view every time the page loads. There are two ways to remove that, and they behave differently for anyone who prefers the tile view.

☐ A — Always start in List Every page load opens in List for everyone. Exactly as requested. The trade-off is that anyone who prefers tiles now switches every time instead.
☐ B — Start in List, then rememberOur recommendation The first visit opens in List. After that the page opens in whichever view that person last used, so nobody keeps switching. The preference is held in the browser, so a different computer starts in List again.

Decision 3

Who does the List default apply to?

Your note said “all logged in users”, which could mean your own team or everyone with a login, including client and partner accounts.

☐ A — Your internal team onlyOur recommendation Analysts and administrators. External and partner logins keep opening on the tile view, which is the presentation your certificate holders are used to.
☐ B — Everyone with a login Including external company and partner accounts. Worth pausing on: the tile view is the polished view those audiences see, so this changes their first impression too.
