Purpose

Your certificate records move through a defined set of statuses over their lifecycle. This document lays out the rules that control which status a certificate may move to next, so you can confirm they match how your business actually works — or tell us what to change. Reviewing this document changes nothing in the system; it is for your verification only.

Background

Earlier this year, as part of a security update, we added rules that govern how a certificate moves from one status to the next — so a record can't accidentally be placed in a state that doesn't reflect where it actually is in the process. Before that update, the edit screen accepted any status change without checking.

The eight statuses themselves were defined by your team. The specific rules for which status may follow which were set by our team during that security update and have not yet been formally reviewed with you. That is the reason for this document.

The scenario that prompted this review
Moving a certificate directly from Draft → Republished is currently blocked. Republished can only be reached from Client Approved – Paid or Expired, because it means a previously-live certificate is being re-issued. This is a rule to confirm — not a system fault. The sign-off section below includes a specific Yes/No line for it.

Automatic exceptions

Three behaviors intentionally override the rules above. Please confirm they are still what you want.

  1. Automatic expiry on save. If a certificate's expiration date is already in the past when it is saved, its status is automatically set to Expired, regardless of the rules above.
  2. Correcting an expiration date. If a certificate expired automatically and you later move its expiration date back to today or the future, the system restores the status it held just before it expired — even where the rules above wouldn't otherwise allow that step. This lets you undo a date-driven expiry.
  3. Overnight expiry check. An automated nightly process moves any active certificate whose expiration date has passed to Expired.

Who can change status

  • Analysts and Administrators may move a certificate to any status permitted by the rules above.
  • External users may only create certificates in Draft; they cannot change status.
A note on the editing experience. When the block first appeared, the edit screen kept your rejected choice selected, which made it feel like you were locked out (nothing was ever lost). This has already been corrected in the upcoming update: the status field now returns to the certificate's real status, and the message lists exactly which statuses you can move to. No action is needed — noted here for completeness.
