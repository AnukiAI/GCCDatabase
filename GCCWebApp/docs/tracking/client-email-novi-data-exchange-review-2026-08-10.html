Novi's preferred method of exchange is a direct connection to your feed on their schedule — which is exactly how the feed already works. Nothing to build there, and you can support the product-level feed Novi prefers rather than the minimum option.

1. How validity is expressed

Our feed answers Novi's status question a different way, by design for Amazon: if a certificate is in the feed it is currently valid; if it is absent, it is not. That works well for product badging. It is less precise for checking a brand's claim, because a certificate that isn't found could be expired, on hold, confidential, or simply a mistyped number — the feed can't say which.

Two things to weigh before you decide:

  • There is no "revoked" status in the system at all. The certificate workflow you approved has eight states and revocation isn't one. Adding it would change the screens your analysts use daily, not just the feed.
  • Certificates marked confidential are paid for but kept private. If we sent every certificate with a status label attached, those would become visible to Novi unless we deliberately exclude them. That's your call, not ours — our recommendation is to keep them hidden.

Also worth knowing: Amazon uses this same feed, so changing what the status value means would require them to review and re-test their side, for a change made on Novi's behalf.

Our recommendation: connect Novi now on the current arrangement, which fully supports badging. If Novi confirms they specifically need the three-way status, we'd scope it as a change and enable it for Novi's connection only, leaving Amazon's untouched.

2. Certification standard version

There is no field for this anywhere, and it wasn't in the original requirements. One question decides whether it is work at all: do your certifications get issued against numbered versions of a published standard? If not, the honest answer to Novi is "not applicable" — no build, no cost. If they do, we'd store the version on each certificate rather than on the certification type, so that updating a standard later doesn't retroactively change what every past certificate reports.

3. Housekeeping — no cost, but needs an owner

Product identifiers can be tagged to a specific partner, and a tagged identifier is hidden from other partners — that isolation is deliberate. The consequence: any identifier tagged only to Amazon will be invisible to Novi, so Novi's view of which products a certificate covers could be narrower than reality, and some genuinely certified products wouldn't be recognised. The fix is a data-entry convention rather than software — identifiers that apply generally should be left untagged — but someone on your side should own it before Novi goes live.
